🤖 Lesson 4: Data Storage & Compression

Syllabus Topic 1.3 — measuring data, calculating file sizes, and making files smaller

Overview
Lesson 4, the final lesson of the Data Representation unit, covers syllabus Topic 1.3 in full. Suggested length: 60–70 minutes. All teaching content is shared; the practice, quiz and exam-style question are split into three self-selected levels (Levels 1–4, 4–7, 7–9). Answers auto-save in the student’s browser, and students send their work as a PDF.

🪜 Using the levels

  • Levels 1–4 (Getting started): single-step questions on units and vocabulary, one-step conversions, and RLE on short strings. Most of the quiz is multiple choice.
  • Levels 4–7 (Getting there): full image and sound file-size calculations to KiB/MiB, encoding and decoding RLE, and choosing lossy or lossless for a given use. The exam question is a 4-mark “explain”.
  • Levels 7–9 (Stretch): stereo, unit mismatches (GiB → MiB), working backwards (finding colour depth or recording length), percentage reduction, and spotting when RLE makes files bigger. The exam question is a 6-mark “evaluate”.
  • Students choose their own level. To build confidence, suggest that less secure students start at Levels 1–4 and move up when the quiz prompts them. Each level saves separately, and the PDF only includes levels the student has attempted, so you can see if they stretched themselves.
  • If you would rather set a level yourself, tell the student which one to complete. The PDF shows clearly which level(s) were done.

💬 Discussion prompts: what to look for

PromptModel answer
1. How many 2.25 MiB photos fit on a 128 GiB phone?128 GiB = 128 × 1024 = 131,072 MiB; 131,072 ÷ 2.25 ≈ 58,254 photos. Assumptions: all storage is free (the operating system and apps take space); every photo is the same size; there is no compression (real phone photos are JPEGs, so far more would fit).
2. Why would lossy be wrong for bank records, and lossless be wrong for streaming?Bank records must be restored exactly. Losing even one digit of an account balance would be unacceptable, so lossless is needed. For streaming, lossless files would be much larger, which means more bandwidth, more buffering and higher costs. Listeners can’t hear the small losses from lossy compression, so lossy is the better choice there.

⚠️ Common misconceptions

  • Using 1000 instead of 1024. Students who have used kB/MB elsewhere often divide by 1000. The 0478 mark scheme expects 1024.
  • Skipping the ÷ 8 step. Students go straight from bits to KiB by dividing by 1024. Insist they write each step in the ladder.
  • Not converting minutes to seconds in sound calculations, and forgetting to double for stereo.
  • Multiplying when they should divide (and the other way round). Use “bigger unit → smaller number → divide”.
  • “Lossless means no compression.” Lossless files are smaller; the point is that the original can be rebuilt exactly.
  • “RLE always makes files smaller.” With few repeated runs, RLE can double the size (ABCD → 1A1B1C1D).
  • Lossy compression described vaguely as “lowering quality”. Exam answers should name a method, e.g. reducing colour depth or resolution, removing sounds outside human hearing, or reducing sample rate or resolution.

🌳 Levels 7–9: Exam-style question [6]

Levels-based marking. Indicative content:

  • Lossy, for: much smaller files, so less bandwidth and faster streaming with less buffering; lower storage and data costs for the company and listeners; it can remove sounds outside human hearing, or quieter sounds masked by louder ones, so most listeners notice little or no difference; it can offer several quality settings.
  • Lossy, against: data is permanently lost, so the original can’t be rebuilt; audiophiles may notice reduced quality; repeated compression degrades the audio.
  • Lossless, for: the original audio is restored exactly, at full quality, which suits a premium or “hi-fi” tier.
  • Lossless, against: files are much larger, which means more bandwidth, more buffering on slow connections and higher costs; RLE-style methods work poorly on complex audio data.
  • Justified conclusion: e.g. lossy as the default because of bandwidth and streaming speed, with lossless offered as an optional high-quality tier.

🚀 Extension ideas

  • Convert the CD-quality stereo song from Lesson 3’s extension (31,752,000 bytes) to MiB: 31,752,000 ÷ 1024² = 30.28 MiB. This matches Stretch practice Q2 and is a nice link to make.
  • Students design a 1-bit 8 × 8 image that RLE compresses well, and one that it compresses badly, then count the characters in each.
  • Research: why do PNG files suit logos and screenshots, but JPEG files suit photographs?
  • Compare 1 TB (as sold by a shop) with TiB: how many GiB do you actually get? 10¹² ÷ 1024³ ≈ 931 GiB.
